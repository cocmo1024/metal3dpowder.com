## Current and long-term demand rationale

Sources checked:

- IEA Direct Air Capture tracker: https://www.iea.org/energy-system/carbon-capture-utilisation-and-storage/direct-air-capture

Why this is topical:

- DAC is moving from small plants and pilots toward larger demonstrations and hub-scale planning.
- DOE and IEA both treat DAC/carbon dioxide removal as an active carbon management category.
- Recent DAC momentum creates near-term search interest, but the powder-search value is long-term: DAC skids, pilot loops, auxiliary manifolds, sensor blocks, and CO2 handling hardware will keep requiring material decisions.

- generic DAC policy commentary

- unsupported qualification, certification, or customer claims
- competing with the IN625 carbon capture amine-service page
- treating 316L as a universal carbon capture material

The article focuses on:

- moderate stainless auxiliary DAC hardware
- where 316L is a practical LPBF route
- where nickel alloy review becomes more defensible
- powder specification and RFQ details
- cleaning, powder removal, leak/vacuum testing, inspection, and post-processing
