Ti64 Grade 23 powder is usually discussed as a medical implant material, but the same ELI-style titanium route can also matter in surgical guides, drill-guide carriers, reusable alignment hardware, and instrument-adjacent components. The search intent is different from a spinal cage or orthopedic implant page: the buyer is often trying to decide whether a metal AM route is justified for a guide, fixture, or instrument component that must survive handling, cleaning, sterilization, and qualification review.

That distinction matters. Not every patient-specific surgical guide should become a titanium part. Many single-use guides are better served by qualified polymers or conventional manufacturing. Ti64 Grade 23 powder becomes relevant when the guide or instrument-adjacent hardware needs metallic stiffness, thin-section strength, wear resistance around sleeves or contact features, repeatable cleaning access, and a controlled titanium documentation trail.

## Where Ti64 Grade 23 powder can fit

Ti64 Grade 23 powder is most defensible when the part needs controlled Ti-6Al-4V ELI logic rather than ordinary structural titanium. In surgical-guide and instrument-adjacent work, that usually means the metal part is doing one or more of these jobs.

| Hardware type | Why Grade 23 may be considered | Powder or process issue to define early |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drill-guide bodies or sleeve carriers | Thin sections, stiffness, repeated handling, and controlled titanium chemistry may matter together. | PSD, oxygen limit, sleeve-bore machining stock, and cleaning access. |
| Cutting or positioning guide frames | Metal can help when low-profile geometry needs stiffness that a polymer route may not provide. | Build orientation, support removal, edge finishing, and contact-surface inspection. |
| Reusable alignment tools | Titanium offers low weight and corrosion-resistant handling when the component is repeatedly cleaned or sterilized. | Surface finish, marking, batch traceability, and post-processing sequence. |
| Hybrid guide components | A printed titanium carrier may hold machined bushings, inserts, or locating features. | Machined interfaces, press-fit zones, distortion after heat treatment, and inspection plan. |
| Qualification coupons and process evidence | Medical-facing programs may need evidence from the same powder/process route. | Powder lot, reuse policy, coupons, test bars, and retained samples. |

For many RFQs, the strongest first answer is not "yes, use Grade 23." The strongest answer is "define why metal is needed, then decide whether Grade 23 is the correct Ti64 route."

## When metal is not the right answer

A technically useful page should also say when the material is not a fit. Ti64 Grade 23 powder should not be forced into every surgical-guide discussion.

Do not start with a Grade 23 powder request when:

- the guide is a low-load, single-use polymer guide with a validated polymer route,
- a machined stainless steel instrument already meets the workflow and cost target,
- the design includes blind cavities or crevices that make powder removal and cleaning unrealistic,
- the project has no defined regulatory, quality, or validation pathway,
- the part needs only generic titanium strength rather than ELI-style chemistry control,
- the quote request cannot define the AM process, PSD, powder certificate, or post-processing plan.

This is especially important for patient-specific hardware. The fact that a part matches anatomy does not by itself justify metal powder bed fusion, and it does not remove medical-device requirements. FDA states that devices manufactured by 3D printing are generally subject to the same regulatory requirements as more traditionally manufactured devices.

## Powder specification points for an RFQ

For LPBF surgical-guide and instrument hardware, the powder inquiry should be specific enough that a supplier can respond technically rather than with a generic titanium quote.

### Grade and standard intent

Ask whether the project needs Ti-6Al-4V ELI / Grade 23 or a broader Ti64 route. ASTM F3001 covers additively manufactured Ti-6Al-4V ELI components made by full-melt powder bed fusion, including laser melting and electron beam melting, and its scope includes requirements around feedstock, process, chemical composition, microstructure, mechanical properties, thermal processing, inspection, certification, marking, packaging, and quality programs. Use that type of standard logic to frame the component route, then align the powder certificate with the actual purchase requirement.

### Particle-size distribution

LPBF titanium powder is commonly requested in fine powder-bed ranges such as 15-45 um or 15-53 um, but the correct range depends on the machine, layer thickness, geometry, and supplier route. Do not copy a PSD from an implant lattice page without checking whether the guide geometry, sleeve features, and required surface quality are different. The [LPBF powder particle-size guide](/posts/Alloys/lpbf-powder-particle-size-guide/) is the better starting point for that decision.

### Oxygen and interstitial control

Grade 23 only has value if the interstitial logic is preserved. Oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, and powder reuse discipline should be part of the request. If the program is still using one broad oxygen limit for every titanium purchase, review the [titanium oxygen-limit discussion guide](/posts/Alloys/titanium-oxygen-limit-discussion-guide/) before freezing the specification.

### Morphology, flowability, and contamination control

Surgical-guide hardware can include thin ribs, small windows, sleeve bosses, and contact pads. Consistent powder spreading matters because local lack-of-fusion risk or surface roughness can become a post-processing and inspection burden. The RFQ should ask for morphology expectations, satellite control, flowability or apparent-density data when relevant, and contamination controls suitable for reactive titanium powder.

### Traceability and powder reuse

For medical-facing work, the powder lot should be traceable. Reuse policy should be explicit: virgin-only, controlled blend, maximum reuse cycles, sieve practice, oxygen monitoring, and retained sample expectations. This is not just paperwork. Powder history can change the ductility and quality argument that made Grade 23 attractive in the first place.

## Design and post-processing details that affect powder choice

The powder decision cannot be separated from the finished component route. For guide and instrument hardware, the following details often decide whether LPBF titanium is realistic.

| Design or post-processing point | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Sleeve bores and drill-guiding surfaces | These should usually be treated as machined or inserted features, not as rough as-built holes. |
| Bone-contact or anatomy-contact surfaces | Surface condition, edge rounding, and inspection need to match the validated device route. |
| Powder removal | Avoid trapped powder in blind channels, nested features, and inaccessible support regions. |
| Cleaning and sterilization workflow | Geometry must allow cleaning validation; powder material alone does not solve that issue. |
| Heat treatment and distortion | Thin guide frames can move during stress relief or HIP; machining stock may be needed. |
| Identification and traceability marking | Marking must be compatible with the quality system and surface requirements. |

The buyer should define these points before sending a powder RFQ. Otherwise, the powder supplier can confirm chemistry and PSD but cannot prevent a poor device design from becoming hard to qualify.

## Grade 23 vs other material routes

Ti64 Grade 23 is only one material path. A strong inquiry compares it against realistic alternatives.

Use Grade 23 when the hardware needs ELI-style titanium logic, controlled chemistry, low weight, corrosion-resistant handling, and a qualification-driven documentation path.

Use standard Ti64 / Grade 5 when the part is structural, weight-sensitive, and titanium is needed, but the tighter Grade 23 chemistry is not justified. The [TC4 / Ti-6Al-4V powder page](/posts/Alloys/tc4-ti6al4v-powder/) is the better fit for that broader decision.

Use commercially pure titanium when corrosion behavior or CP titanium logic matters more than Ti64-level strength. For that path, compare against [TA1 commercially pure titanium powder](/posts/Alloys/ta1-cp-titanium-powder/) and [TA1 chemical and biomedical titanium parts](/posts/Alloys/ta1-cp-titanium-powder/).

Use cobalt chrome when the application is dental or wear-critical rather than a titanium guide or instrument frame. The dental route is covered in the [cobalt chrome vs titanium powder dental AM comparison](/posts/Alloys/cocrmo-powder-dental-frameworks-and-crowns/).

## RFQ checklist for surgical-guide and instrument hardware

A useful Ti64 Grade 23 powder inquiry for this application should include:

- part type: surgical guide, drill-guide carrier, sleeve holder, reusable alignment tool, or instrument component,
- whether the part is patient-specific, surgeon-specific, reusable, or development hardware,
- target AM process: LPBF, EBM, or another route,
- target powder PSD and machine family,
- required grade: Ti-6Al-4V ELI / Grade 23 or standard Ti64,
- chemistry limits, especially oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, and iron,
- powder lot traceability and reuse policy,
- expected build orientation, critical features, and minimum wall or rib thickness,
- surfaces that will be machined after printing,
- sleeve, bushing, or insert interfaces,
- cleaning and powder-removal constraints,
- heat treatment, HIP, finishing, passivation, or inspection expectations,
- documentation level needed for prototype, validation, or recurring production.

When those details are included, the conversation moves from "send titanium powder price" to a qualified Grade 23 powder discussion that can support real medical-facing hardware development.

## Practical takeaway

Ti64 Grade 23 powder is a strong candidate for surgical guides and instrument-adjacent hardware only when the application can justify metal AM, controlled ELI titanium chemistry, and a documented powder/process route. The highest-value inquiry will define the part type, medical workflow, LPBF powder range, oxygen limit, post-processing plan, and validation expectations before asking for price.
