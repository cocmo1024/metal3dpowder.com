Ti64 Grade 23 powder becomes important when the project is not just asking for titanium, but for a more controlled ELI-style titanium route suited to medical hardware. These parts usually care about ductility, toughness, biocompatibility, and tighter chemistry intent more than they care about squeezing maximum strength out of a standard Ti64 route.

## Where Grade 23 usually enters

This route is most relevant for:

- orthopedic implants,
- spinal devices,
- medical titanium components,
- controlled medical-development hardware.

These are not generic titanium applications. They are applications where the titanium grade definition matters early.

If the project is not an implant but a surgical guide, drill-guide carrier, reusable alignment tool, or instrument-adjacent component, use the narrower [Ti64 Grade 23 surgical guide and instrument hardware page](/posts/Alloys/ti64-grade-23-powder-surgical-guides-and-instrument-hardware/) instead of treating every medical-facing titanium part as an implant inquiry.

## Why Grade 23 is different from standard Ti64

Grade 23 stays inside the Ti-6Al-4V family, but with lower oxygen and iron than a standard Ti64 route. That usually means better elongation and toughness with somewhat lower strength. For medical and implant-side reasoning, that trade is often the point.

## Why this is a high-value powder topic

Medical titanium programs are rarely casual. The project is usually already thinking about:

- grade intent,
- release logic,
- biocompatibility-driven material choice,
- whether the route should stay inside Ti64 ELI rather than broad titanium language.

That makes Grade 23 one of the strongest titanium powder topics for authority-building content.

## When not to force Grade 23

If the part is really an aerospace or industrial structural component without medical-grade titanium logic, a broader [TC4 / Ti64 powder route](/posts/Alloys/tc4-ti6al4v-powder/) may be enough. Grade 23 should be used when the application actually needs the tighter titanium definition.

## The practical takeaway

Use Ti64 Grade 23 powder when the application is an orthopedic implant or medical titanium component that needs a tougher, cleaner ELI-style Ti64 route. ## Orthopedic implants, spinal cages, and porous structures

These terms now belong to one implant-side powder decision. The geometry may differ, but all require an exact Grade 23/ELI material basis, controlled powder genealogy, a validated build and post-process route, and device-specific evidence.

| Device geometry | AM value | Main validation burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid orthopedic implant component | Patient or design-specific geometry and consolidation | Mechanical, fatigue, surface, cleaning, sterilization and device requirements |
| Spinal cage | Porous or lattice features and anatomical geometry | Strut geometry, defects, fatigue, subsidence-related design evidence, cleaning |
| Porous implant structure | Controlled pore architecture for the device design | Pore/strut inspection, trapped powder, surface, mechanical and biological evaluation |
| Surgical guide or instrument hardware | Custom fit or integrated features | Different regulatory, cleaning, sterilization, wear and dimensional route |

The separate surgical guide and instrument page remains because non-implant hardware can have a different device and acceptance path.

## Grade 23 powder control

State the referenced Grade 23 specification and chemistry, especially interstitial limits. Request production route, lot identity, PSD and method, morphology, virgin/reused status, contamination controls, packaging, and change notification. Powder reuse and sieving should be part of the validated manufacturing process rather than an undocumented assumption.

Porous geometry adds powder-removal and inspection burdens. Define minimum pore and strut, build orientation, cleaning, residue acceptance, internal inspection, surface state, heat treatment, HIP if used, and representative mechanical testing.

## RFQ fields

Include the device class and geometry, Grade 23 specification, process and machine, PSD, quantity, powder condition, interstitial limits, document package, cleaning and inspection needs, and regulatory market. Request the lot COA, PSD report, TDS/SDS, and available SEM or morphology evidence. The responsible medical-device manufacturer must establish final device safety and compliance.
