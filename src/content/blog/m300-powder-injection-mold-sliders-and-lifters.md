M300 powder deserves a separate discussion for injection mold sliders and lifters because these parts are not just another mold insert. A slider or lifter has to move, locate, release an undercut, survive repeated cycles, hold machined datum surfaces, and fit into a tight tool envelope. That makes the powder decision more specific than a broad "M300 mold tooling" question.

The current manufacturing context keeps this topic relevant. The [PLASTICS Size and Impact report](https://www.plasticsindustry.org/data-analysis-reports/size-impact-plastics-economy/) continues to track plastics manufacturing as a large industrial base with ongoing forecast work, while the long-term search value is stable: production molds keep needing better cooling, more compact moving details, and faster replacement of high-value inserts. M300 powder can fit that problem when LPBF changes the slider or lifter geometry enough to justify the route.

## Why sliders and lifters are different from general inserts

Injection mold sliders and lifters often solve undercut, side-action, ejection, and local release problems. They are judged by fit, movement, repeatability, and part quality, not only by hardness. A normal core insert may sit in one location and control a cavity feature. A slider or lifter may move on an angle, carry a forming surface, support a wear land, and return accurately every cycle.

That changes the M300 discussion. The part may need:

- post-aged strength and hardness
- stable machined datum faces
- accurate angled or cam-driven motion surfaces
- local cooling near undercut, rib, clip, boss, or thick-wall features
- wear-aware contact zones and replaceable inserts
- clear machining stock on shutoff, forming, and guide surfaces

This is why a slider or lifter is not the same search intent as a general [M300 mold tooling insert](/posts/Alloys/m300-powder-mold-tooling-inserts/) or a broad [M300 core insert](/posts/Alloys/m300-powder-mold-tooling-inserts/) page.

## Where M300 powder can fit

M300 is often strongest when the molding detail is compact, precision-driven, and valuable enough that LPBF geometry matters. Useful candidates can include:

- compact sliders with localized cooling near thick features or undercuts
- lifter bodies with complex angled geometry and hard-to-machine internal features
- side-action inserts that combine forming geometry, coolant routing, and mounting features
- replaceable shutoff or undercut details where insert replacement is part of the tooling plan
- cam-driven blocks that need local reinforcement and defined machining surfaces
- development tooling where the team is iterating a difficult release or cooling issue before committing to a full tool redesign

The additive value is not "print every slider." It appears when the slider or lifter has geometry that conventional machining handles poorly: hidden coolant routes, compact features around a difficult undercut, part consolidation, or a replaceable detail that can be rebuilt without redesigning the whole mold.

## What LPBF changes for moving mold details

[NIST describes laser powder bed fusion](https://www.nist.gov/video/3d-printing-laser-and-metal-powder) as a process where metal powder is spread in layers and a laser melts each layer into the part. For M300 sliders and lifters, that layer-based route can help with geometry, but it also creates tooling-specific responsibilities.

LPBF can help when the tool needs:

- coolant passages closer to the forming surface than drilling allows
- shorter thermal paths around ribs, bosses, hooks, clips, or side features
- a compact moving block with internal features that would otherwise need several machined parts
- a repeatable replacement insert where build orientation, machining datum, and heat treatment are documented
- a slider or lifter body that benefits from local weight reduction without weakening contact faces

LPBF does not remove the need for machining. Shutoff surfaces, guide surfaces, angled lifter faces, wear lands, fitting surfaces, dowel holes, screw holes, and cavity-forming areas usually need finishing. The powder and build plan should reserve material for those operations.

## How this differs from compact mold inserts

The existing M300 compact mold insert page is about recurring production inserts where cooling geometry and insert replacement justify M300. This page is narrower because sliders and lifters add motion.

That motion changes the RFQ:

- the drawing must separate as-built areas from machined sliding or shutoff areas
- the heat-treatment route must fit dimensional stability expectations
- the toolmaker must understand which faces locate, move, rub, seal, or form the molded part
- surface finish and hardness targets should be defined by function, not applied uniformly
- any internal cooling needs powder-removal and cleaning access

In other words, a slider or lifter needs the powder supplier, printer, and moldmaker to understand the component's movement logic.

## M300 vs H13 for injection mold sliders and lifters

M300 and H13 are both tooling steels, but they answer different mold problems. Use M300 when the tool detail is precision-driven, compact, insert-like, and benefits from post-aged strength and LPBF cooling geometry. Use H13 when the part is more heat-driven, more hot-work-like, or when thermal fatigue and heat checking dominate the risk.

The [H13 vs M300 powder guide](/posts/Alloys/h13-vs-m300-powder/) is the broad comparison. For slider and lifter work, the decision usually starts with these questions:

| Question | M300 usually fits when... | H13 usually fits when... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main duty | Precision moving insert, undercut release, compact tooling detail | Hotter production tool, stronger thermal fatigue concern |
| AM value | Internal cooling, compact geometry, post-aged strength | Hot-work durability plus conformal cooling |
| Critical surfaces | Datum, guide, shutoff, forming, and wear faces need machining | Thermal-cycling areas and hot-work surfaces dominate |
| Risk | Dimensional stability after aging and finishing | Heat checking, thermal cycling, and production heat load |

If the project is mainly an injection molding production tool with heat-driven durability requirements, review the [H13 injection molding conformal cooling page](/posts/Alloys/h13-powder-conformal-cooling-inserts/). If the job is a precision moving insert with compact cooling and controlled finishing, M300 is often the cleaner first discussion.

## RFQ checklist for M300 slider and lifter powder projects

A useful RFQ should describe the tool detail, not only the alloy. Include:

- alloy expectation: M300, 1.2709 maraging steel, or project-specific equivalent
- process route: LPBF, development insert, production insert, replacement detail, or validation build
- powder details: PSD, chemistry, oxygen, morphology, flowability, apparent density, lot traceability, COA, and reuse policy
- slider or lifter function: side action, undercut release, angled lifter, cam-driven movement, shutoff detail, or forming insert
- movement and contact surfaces: guide faces, angled faces, wear lands, dowel locations, screw locations, and shutoff surfaces
- cooling intent: channel location, cleaning access, powder-removal access, pressure test, and leak-test expectation
- heat treatment: aging condition, dimensional control, hardness target, and whether coupons are needed
- machining plan: datum faces, finishing stock, wire EDM, grinding, polishing, texture, and cavity-surface requirements
- production context: cycle count target, resin family, glass-fiber content, mold temperature, cooling medium, and replacement strategy

For the broader powder checklist, use the [metal powder specification guide](/posts/Alloys/metal-powder-specification-guide-psd-oxygen-flowability/). For process sizing and LPBF assumptions, use the [LPBF powder particle size guide](/posts/Alloys/lpbf-powder-particle-size-guide/).

## The practical takeaway

Use M300 powder for injection mold sliders and lifters when the component is a precision moving mold detail with compact geometry, machined datum surfaces, controlled aging, and enough LPBF value to justify a printed route. Do not use it as a blanket replacement for every slider, lifter, or standard mold component.

The strongest inquiry defines the movement, contact faces, cooling intent, heat treatment, machining stock, powder specification, and replacement strategy together. That is how a M300 powder request becomes a serious mold tooling conversation rather than a generic maraging steel quote.
