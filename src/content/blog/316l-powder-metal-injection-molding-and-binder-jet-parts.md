316L powder matters well beyond LPBF. In metal injection molding and binder jet production, 316L remains one of the most useful stainless routes when the part needs corrosion resistance, ductility, and a production-friendly stainless chemistry.

## Why 316L sits in the high-volume bucket

This route is commonly tied to:

- medical stainless parts,
- automotive and industrial stainless hardware,
- corrosion-side stainless components,
- filters and fine stainless production parts.

The powder discussion is usually less about AM novelty and more about repeatable stainless powder for scalable production.

## Why 316L works in MIM and binder jet

The logic is similar to LPBF at the alloy level: 316L is chosen when corrosion resistance and general stainless usability matter more than precipitation-hardening strength. In MIM and binder jet, that makes it one of the natural first routes for production stainless hardware.

## Where this differs from LPBF use

A 316L LPBF project is often thinking about geometry freedom. A 316L MIM or binder jet program is more likely thinking about:

- production scale,
- sintering route,
- isotropic stainless properties,
- corrosion-side end use.

That difference matters because the same alloy is being considered in a different production context.

## When 17-4PH takes over

If the stainless part is strength-driven rather than corrosion-driven, [17-4PH](/posts/Alloys/17-4ph-stainless-steel-powder/) may be the better route. The powder choice should still follow the real service condition and the production method.

## The practical takeaway

Use 316L powder when the job is a metal injection molding or binder jet stainless part that values corrosion resistance, ductility, and scalable stainless production. For the LPBF route, return to the main [316L powder guide](/posts/Alloys/316l-stainless-steel-powder/). For stronger stainless production parts, compare against [17-4PH powder](/posts/Alloys/17-4ph-powder-metal-injection-molding-and-binder-jet-parts/). Filters and other corrosion-side MIM parts are covered on this consolidated page; define pore or filtration target, sintered density, corrosion environment, cleaning, dimensional change, and acceptance evidence in the RFQ.

## MIM, binder jet, and functional porosity

316L powder can support dense sintered hardware, intentionally porous filters, or corrosion-side production parts, but those outcomes require different powder and acceptance strategies.

| Target | Powder/process priority | Final evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dense MIM part | Feedstock consistency, debinding, sintering shrinkage and chemistry | Density, dimensions, chemistry, mechanical and corrosion basis |
| Dense binder-jet part | Spreading, binder interaction, depowdering, sintering and distortion | Density distribution, dimensions, defects, final properties |
| Porous filter or flow element | Controlled pore structure rather than maximum density | Pore/flow method, integrity, cleanliness, corrosion and shedding |
| General corrosion-side small part | Repeatable scalable stainless route | Surface, dimensions, chemistry and service-specific test |

Do not use LPBF PSD rules automatically. Fine distributions may improve packing but can change cohesion, binder behavior, handling, exposure, debinding, and sintering. The feedstock supplier and production platform should define the validated range and test basis.

## 316L versus 17-4PH

316L is normally reviewed where corrosion resistance and ductility lead. 17-4PH is reviewed where higher final strength or hardness justifies a controlled precipitation-hardening route. For porous filters, the alloy and pore target should be selected together; “316L powder” does not establish filtration performance.

## RFQ fields

Provide the exact 316L basis, MIM or binder-jet platform, powder/feedstock form, PSD method, binder system where known, quantity, target density or porosity, shrinkage strategy, final thermal route, corrosion environment, dimensions, and required documents. Request the COA, PSD data, TDS/SDS, morphology or SEM evidence, and representative sintered-part results for the actual process.
