IN718 powder is worth reviewing for gas turbine fuel nozzle bodies, combustor swirler hardware, injector-adjacent supports, and small hot-section flow parts when the project is strength-led, temperature-loaded, geometry-sensitive, and tied to a controlled LPBF plus heat-treatment route. The strongest fit is not every combustor part. It is compact nickel hardware where internal passages, fuel or air distribution, mounting interfaces, thermal-mechanical load, and inspection access all matter together.

This topic has current search value because gas turbines are moving back into a broader power conversation. The [IEA's 2026 electricity outlook](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/executive-summary) forecasts strong global electricity demand growth through 2030, while its [Energy and AI analysis](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-supply-for-ai) projects data centre electricity supply growing from 460 TWh in 2024 to over 1 000 TWh in 2030. The IEA also notes that some US data centre projects are considering onsite gas-fired generation and that gas turbine supply is a constraint in that path. In the United States, the [EIA expects natural gas consumption for power generation to reach a new summer record in 2027](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67725), with data centers and large manufacturing facilities contributing to commercial demand growth.

The long-term powder-search value is more specific than the energy headline. Gas turbines, aero engines, hydrogen-capable combustion development, supercritical CO2 cycles, and industrial turbomachinery all keep asking the same engineering question: when does a nickel alloy powder route help a small high-temperature part that is difficult to machine, braze, weld, clean, and inspect?

## Why fuel nozzles and swirlers point to powder AM

Fuel nozzle and combustor swirler hardware is usually a geometry problem before it is a marketing topic. The part may need small passages, mixing features, air or fuel distribution holes, swirl features, mounting bosses, seal faces, and local wall transitions inside a tight envelope. Conventional fabrication can work, but it may require several machined, brazed, or welded pieces.

GE Aerospace's well-known additive fuel nozzle example is useful as an AM geometry reference because GE reported that a fuel nozzle tip previously made from about 20 welded and brazed pieces could be printed as one part with significant weight reduction. That example should not be treated as an IN718 material claim; GE has separately described the LEAP injector material as cobalt chromium. For this page, the point is narrower: fuel nozzle geometry is a real additive manufacturing use case, and each program still has to choose the powder alloy from its own temperature, oxidation, strength, and inspection requirements.

IN718 enters the review when the component is more structural and strength-retention led than oxidation-led. The part may sit around a fuel injection module, swirler assembly, pilot injector body, burner test rig, combustor support feature, or turbine-adjacent hot-section flow path where precipitation-strengthened nickel behavior is useful.

## Where IN718 powder can fit

IN718 powder is usually most credible for stationary or stationary-adjacent hardware where the final route can support heat treatment, machining, inspection, and release evidence. Candidate part families may include:

- gas turbine fuel nozzle bodies with compact mounting and internal routing
- pilot injector housings and fuel-distribution blocks in development hardware
- combustor swirler supports, swirl-cup-adjacent hardware, and air-distribution features
- injector-adjacent brackets, bosses, and reinforced mounting ears
- small hot-section manifolds that combine fuel, purge, or cooling paths
- burner-rig and engine-development test parts with defined temperature and flow limits
- non-rotating turbine-adjacent flow hardware that needs high-temperature strength
- qualification coupons and comparison builds printed from the same IN718 powder lot

The additive value is usually part consolidation, controlled routing, smaller packaging, fewer joints, or faster development iteration. If the component is a simple tube, plate, flange, or conventional combustor liner, the powder route may not add enough value.

## When Hastelloy X or IN625 may be cleaner

IN718 should not be forced into every combustor or hot-gas part. If oxidation resistance, flame adjacency, and hot-gas durability dominate the design, [Hastelloy X powder](/posts/Alloys/hastelloy-x-powder/) or the [Hastelloy X hydrogen burner and combustor guide](/posts/Alloys/hastelloy-x-powder-hydrogen-burner-and-combustor-hardware/) may be the better starting point. If corrosion, chemical media, or aggressive cleaning chemistry dominates, [IN625 powder](/posts/Alloys/gh3625-in625-powder/) may be more defensible.

Use the [IN718 vs Hastelloy X turbine and furnace comparison](/posts/Alloys/in718-vs-hastelloy-x-powder-turbine-vs-furnace-hardware/) when the project is stuck between structural hot-side strength and oxidation-driven hot-gas service. Use the [IN718 vs IN625 powder comparison](/posts/Alloys/in718-vs-in625-powder/) when the decision is structural strength versus corrosion-led nickel alloy behavior.

The short rule is simple: IN718 fits better when precipitation-strengthened hot-side strength is central. Hastelloy X fits better when oxidation and flame-adjacent hot-gas exposure are central. IN625 fits better when corrosion and chemical durability are central.

## LPBF details that matter for nozzle and swirler hardware

LPBF is the most natural powder route for small IN718 fuel nozzle and swirler hardware because it can produce compact passages, thin features, multi-directional ports, and integrated bosses. But LPBF also adds acceptance questions that cannot be ignored.

Important checks include:

- powder size range, morphology, chemistry, oxygen, cleanliness, and lot traceability
- minimum passage diameter and whether powder can be removed after printing
- downskin quality, unsupported features, and internal surface condition
- build orientation relative to small holes, thin walls, and seal faces
- machining allowance for threads, gasket lands, fuel interfaces, bosses, and datum faces
- heat treatment, stress relief, and HIP expectations where required
- CT, borescope, leak, pressure, flow, and dimensional inspection
- whether flow test coupons use the same powder lot, heat treatment, and surface condition

For general powder requirements, use the [metal powder specification guide](/posts/Alloys/metal-powder-specification-guide-psd-oxygen-flowability/). For the powder-bed side, use the [LPBF particle size guide](/posts/Alloys/lpbf-powder-particle-size-guide/). In nozzle and swirler hardware, the drawing should also define cleaning and verification paths for internal features.

## What to include in an IN718 fuel nozzle or swirler RFQ

A useful inquiry should do more than name IN718 or GH4169. For gas turbine fuel nozzle bodies, swirler hardware, and combustor-adjacent flow parts, include:

- engine or rig context: aero engine, industrial gas turbine, microturbine, burner rig, hydrogen blend test, or power-generation development hardware
- part class: fuel nozzle body, pilot injector housing, swirler support, air-distribution ring, purge manifold, test coupon, or non-pressure support part
- service condition: temperature range, pressure, vibration, thermal cycling, fuel type, purge gas, combustion environment, and shutdown condition
- failure mode concern: strength retention, fatigue, oxidation, creep, distortion, leakage, flow uniformity, or inspection access
- AM route: LPBF, comparison build, development coupon, or conventional benchmark
- powder specification: IN718 chemistry, PSD, oxygen, morphology, flowability, cleanliness, COA, COC, and lot traceability
- post-processing: stress relief, solution and aging route, HIP if required, machining, surface finishing, cleaning, and packaging
- acceptance evidence: dimensional report, CT, borescope, leak test, pressure test, flow test, metallography, coupon data, and heat-treatment records

Those details help a supplier decide whether IN718 powder is a rational route or whether the part should move toward Hastelloy X, IN625, cobalt alloy, conventional fabrication, or a different design.

## The practical takeaway

Use IN718 powder for gas turbine fuel nozzle bodies and combustor swirler hardware when the job combines high-temperature strength, controlled heat treatment, compact internal geometry, post-machined interfaces, and enough inspection discipline to make LPBF credible.

Do not use IN718 as a blanket combustor material. If the real risk is oxidation-dominated hot-gas exposure, review Hastelloy X. If the real risk is corrosion or chemical media, review IN625. The strongest RFQ names the part class, service condition, internal passages, powder specification, post-processing route, and acceptance evidence before asking for a quote.
